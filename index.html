02 / The big idea

Your brain isn’t broken.
It was built for speed.

That speed is useful. It helps you move through a complicated world without analyzing every detail from scratch. But the same shortcuts can quietly shape what you notice, remember, believe, and decide.

The goal isn’t to become perfectly rational. It’s to recognize when your brain may be taking a shortcut—and know what to do about it.

09 / Questions, answered

Before you
take the first step.

Do I need a background in psychology?

No. The book is written as an approachable field guide for curious adults, not as an academic textbook.

Is the book practical or mostly theoretical?

Each chapter moves from a real scenario to what your brain is doing, why the shortcut exists, warning signs, and a practical technique for countering it.

What topics does it cover?

It covers perception, confirmation bias, anchoring, availability, Dunning–Kruger, sunk cost, hindsight, negativity, the halo effect, memory, optical illusions, social and emotional biases, and more.

What is the 30-day bias-busting plan?

It is the practical closing section of the book: a structured way to turn the ideas into an ongoing personal practice.

How long does it take to read?

Read at your own pace. The structure is designed to let you work through roughly one chapter at a time.
